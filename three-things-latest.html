The SEC Lets Tokenised Shares Trade Without an Exchange Licence

The Securities and Exchange Commission has issued Order 34-106402, the Innovation Exemption. It gives venues trading tokenised shares a five year conditional exemption from the Exchange Act definition of an exchange, so they can run permissioned automated market makers in tokenised National Market System stock without registering as an exchange or a broker dealer. Only tokens carrying the same rights as the ordinary share qualify, and trading halts when the underlying stock halts. The order took effect immediately.

Why this matters

Tokenised shares can now trade in America without an exchange licence, so custody, settlement and best execution questions land on UK desks well before any UK rule does. S&P Global has agreed to buy OpenZeppelin, whose contract libraries have carried more than £27.7tn ($37tn) of cumulative value transferred since 2015.

2

Bank of England Pauses Its Gilt Sales for Six Months

The Monetary Policy Committee held Bank Rate at 3.75 per cent on a 6 to 3 vote and voted unanimously to take its monetary policy gilt holdings to zero by 2034. Of £488bn held, £120bn stays outside the programme to back banknote issuance, £222bn runs off at maturity and £146bn is to be sold, an average reduction of £46bn a year, down from £70bn. All active sales are paused for six months while officials consult HM Treasury on selling long gilts straight to the Debt Management Office.

Why this matters

Long gilt supply is on hold for six months, so treasury desks planning issuance, collateral and hedges are working off a different curve from the one they modelled. Nothing about the sale to the Debt Management Office is settled, and the minutes leave open where monetary action ends and fiscal action begins.

3

Researchers Reached OpenAI's Code Repository Through a Forum Image

Three researchers at Hacktron AI reached OpenAI's internal code repository from a public forum in under 72 hours. A crafted image uploaded to the company's community forum hit an unpatched flaw in an image library and gave them remote code execution. A misconfigured single sign on let them take over an employee's accounts, and that employee's connected Codex session opened the door to the openai/openai monorepo. The work was disclosed responsibly. OpenAI says only limited read access occurred and confirmed a fix about 14 hours later.

Why this matters

A supplier's own code repository was reached through its community forum, an exposure third-party risk reviews almost never ask about. One detail is awkward: Anthropic's Claude Opus 4.8 could not build a reliable exploit with memory randomisation switched on, and Opus 5, released mid-investigation, produced a working one within hours.
