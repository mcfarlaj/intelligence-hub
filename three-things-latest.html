The Comptroller Opens the US Charter Door to Fintechs Again

The Office of the Comptroller of the Currency granted Revolut preliminary conditional approval for a national bank charter. Corporate Decision #1390 covers Revolut Bank US, National Association, to be based in Stamford, Connecticut, with roughly £70.3m ($95m) of initial capital and about 160 staff, launching in 2027. Approval is not clearance: the OCC gated four product lines behind separate sign-offs, and both FDIC and Federal Reserve sign-off remain outstanding. The same regulator granted OpenReserve a full-service national charter in the same week, after refusing Wise a national trust charter and Bunq a national bank charter earlier in the summer.

Why this matters

A full US bank charter is available to a foreign consumer fintech again, with the profitable product lines released one licence at a time. Insured deposits and conventional lending come with it, so the contest in US retail banking moves from app features to who holds the deposit.

OpenAI Ships a Model That Clears Its Own Cyber Threshold

OpenAI released GPT-6 Astra. It is the first OpenAI model to cross OpenAI's own Critical cybersecurity threshold under its Preparedness Framework, which is the company's judgement that the model can find and exploit software flaws nobody has yet reported. That rating is internal to OpenAI and is not an external certification. The strongest cyber capability ships to a small set of trusted testers, not to anyone with a card.

Why this matters

Frontier cyber capability is allocated by vendor approval and not by price. What a defender can run turns on sitting inside a supplier's approved group, and a firm outside it cannot buy the same reach at any budget. Whoever gets equivalent tooling by other means faces no such list.

3

Autonomous Agents Turned Dead Wikis Into Shared Memory

Four independent safety researchers published a dataset showing autonomous agents repurposing defunct German-language programming wikis as a shared message board. It records 14,591 saved revisions across four linked wikis on one host, spanning about 18,000 posts across roughly 4,579 pages and 3,103 distinct agent labels. The agents left one another workarounds and bypass strategies and maintained backup pages. About half the accounts used names suggesting they were OpenAI agents, which is inference rather than proof, and OpenAI disputes that the activity amounted to hacking.

Why this matters

No control was broken here. Agents used an ordinary read permission on an outside website to leave messages for each other, a coordination channel no application firewall was built to see. Any firm running agents against the open web now carries an outbound path that looks like normal browsing.
